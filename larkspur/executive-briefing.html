Executive briefing · Week 4 · 5 minutes

A $5.4M option that protects 2028 earnings

A flood at Larkspur's only beverage co-packer costs about $37.3M of FY2027 EBITDA but does not threaten the covenant. The exposure it leaves is the next year: no stock on hand going into the 2028 hurricane season. A second aseptic site, decided by 15 November 2026, closes that gap at roughly no net cost if the flood comes, and about $5.4M over two years if it does not.

To Priya Natarajan, Chief Financial Officer · cc Marcus Webb, COO · From Windward Risk Advisors · 9 October 2026 · working draft

~20 sec. Open with the answer, in your language. One decision this quarter: start qualifying a second aseptic co-packer by mid-November. Think of it as an option: about five million of EBITDA over two years if nothing happens, roughly free if the flood comes, and it protects 2028. Five minutes on the story behind it.

The lines come back in late November, but there is no spare capacity to rebuild stock. Insurance pays, but a quarter late.

3 · Expected vs unexpectedThe surprise is in the outcome we were hoping for

Expected reaction

The shelf comes back

Retailers wait, the review closes once fill recovers. FY2027 EBITDA −⁠$37.3M, FY2028 +⁠$10.0M as insurance lands.

But the lines restart full, so Larkspur enters the 2028 hurricane season with 0 days of stock. The next stop, even a small one, empties shelves on day one.

Unexpected reaction

Lakemont drops the line

At its February 2028 business review, Lakemont delists the Gonzales lines from 1 March: 31% of their revenue. FY2028 EBITDA −⁠$16.2M.

Volume falls to 14.1M cases, under the 15.0M-case minimum in the co-packer contract. The shortfall charge is not in the records we have.

Neither path breaks the covenant: the October 2027 test reads 2.70x against 3.50x, with $65.2M of headroom. We expected the money to be the problem. Played through, it is the empty buffer.

MODEL, run P-20261009-02 against the central projection. Delisting date and length: ASSUMPTION; February review date: RECORD (SLA Schedule 6-A).

~60 sec. Two ways the story can end. Expected: the shelf comes back. Unexpected: Lakemont, your largest retailer, drops the line. The delisting costs more money. But here's what surprised us: the outcome everyone hopes for is the fragile one, because you restart with zero stock heading into the next hurricane season. And no path breaks the covenant. That is a finding in itself; it overturns what we told you in Week 3.

Under the flood scenario, choosing to qualify a second aseptic site by 15 November 2026 turns 0 days of stock into 27 for the 2028 hurricane season at roughly no net cost to FY2027–28 earnings, because the second site keeps about a fifth of the volume moving while Gonzales is down and frees the capacity to rebuild stock after restart.

MODEL, run P-20261009-03. Site cost ($2.5M opex, $3.5M capex), 4% running premium and 6 to 9 month qualification: ASSUMPTION (not in the pack; Art. 14.2 records 6 to 9 months for a line at the same facility).

~60 sec. The response we tested is A2: move the volume above the co-packer minimum, about a fifth, to a second qualified site. With no flood it's an insurance premium of about five million over two years. In the flood it roughly pays for itself and turns zero days of stock into twenty-seven. If a second storm then hits in 2028, that's the difference between seven million lost and nothing. Timing matters most: ready by May, it's worth three and a half million; slip the decision by three months and it costs four.

6 · Limits and the askWhat A2 cannot do, and what would change the answer

What it cannot do

  • Stop the category review. To hold fill at 96% with Gonzales down, a second site would need about 93% of the volume: full dual sourcing.
  • Prevent a delisting, or the co-packer shortfall charge that follows one.

Facts that could change the answer

  • A real quote and candidate site (our cost figures are assumptions).
  • The standby rate for volume under the 15.0M-case minimum (contract Schedule 4).
  • How the October covenant test measures net debt.
  • The launch volume plan that fills Gonzales to 100%.
The ask

Ask the Treasurer and Procurement to bring back two second-site quotes, the shortfall rate and the covenant test mechanics within five weeks, so the go or no-go is taken by 15 November 2026. Budget at stake if approved: about $6.0M of one-time cost (opex plus capex, our assumption) and a running premium on a fifth of Gonzales volume.

Every month of delay moves the ready date past the August 2027 peak and turns A2 from break-even into a cost.

Coverage figure: MODEL, run P-20261009-03. Missing facts listed in the run records.

~30 sec. Two honest limits. A2 doesn't stop the category review; only near-full dual sourcing does that. And four facts could change the answer, the biggest being a real quote. So the ask is small: five weeks to get quotes and confirm those facts, and a go or no-go by mid-November. Waiting is the expensive option.
