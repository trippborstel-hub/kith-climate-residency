Why a version 2Your records changed the picture in three ways

The largest exposures are not fieldsThe 18 September proposal organised the work around crops. Ranked by money at stake against buffer and cover, the first dependency is a single contract manufacturer and the fourth is a loan covenant. Only two of the top five are growing regions.
The calibration data is not in the recordsWe asked for five to ten years of monthly purchase data and daily point-of-sale sales. The pack holds two years of division results, contract excerpts, an insurance summary and a credit agreement. Calibration now runs on 2021 to 2023 incident actuals we will request, not on long series.
The nearest decisions belong to financeThe board minutes date them: the September insurance submission, the lender's Q3 questionnaire, the October covenant test, the launch in March 2027. The Treasurer and General Counsel own those, so the proposal is now addressed jointly to the COO and the CFO.

Confirmed from your memo: three businesses on three clocks (Produce ships within three days of pack, Beverage carries 27 days of finished goods, Ingredients 38); redundancy on paper that is not independent (Oregon growers sat under the same 2021 heat dome as Yakima; two co-packers but only one aseptic); substitution that is not free (retailer service credits, delisting exposure, customer approval); knowledge that lives in contracts (one grower form carries a 70% clause the other does not).

Nothing here predicts an event. It says what breaks, how the break travels to the statements, and which facts would change the answer.

Change 3Beverage demand stays, bounded, with the synthetic test disclosed

First workstream to drop or defer; needs daily point-of-sale sales by store. Now: a small, fixed-effort workstream that answers one question: does heat move beverage sales enough to change what is stocked where, and when?

What the records contain. No point-of-sale data, no store lists, no customer geography. Beverage revenue by line for one year, and the named retail accounts.

How it runs until retailer data arrives. On a synthetic daily sales set built on real NOAA station weather for ten metros, reconciled to your FY2025 beverage totals, with the heat response written down in advance as an answer key. Its only purpose is to prove that the calibration step can recover a known signal after controlling for weekday, season, promotions and the school calendar. No conclusion about Larkspur's demand is drawn from synthetic sales, and every page that shows it says so.

Limit. A fixed share of effort. If point-of-sale data from Lakemont or Fairmount has not arrived by the week-4 checkpoint, the workstream stops with the method note as its output and the effort moves to the supply and operations tiers. Fee unchanged either way.

  • Assurability. Every figure traces to a document in your pack or a public dataset with provenance. Scenario arithmetic sits in a model file your Treasurer's team can read and re-run. Audit trail is not assurance; we advise, we do not assure.
  • Honesty about limits. No probability from three models. No forecast for a given year. No attribution of a past season to climate change. A null result is reported. "Your buffers hold" is an acceptable answer.
  • Why Windward. Built in Claude Code so it lives in your environment. We read all twenty documents in your pack and found the co-packer and the covenant before the first meeting. We work with your Treasurer and your broker as readily as with procurement.
