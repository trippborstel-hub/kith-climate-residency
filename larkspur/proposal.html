What we heard

Larkspur runs three businesses with different tolerances for disruption: crops from growers in several regions, ingredients and fresh products made in your own plants and by contract manufacturers, and beverages. Retail commitments hold regardless of harvest. You already have studies describing conditions in growing regions and at facilities, and a disclosure process that runs separately from operating decisions.

Those studies do not tell procurement whether to renew a grower, a plant whether to shift allocation, or commercial what to promise. When a question comes up it crosses three teams, often needs new specialist work, and the decision has moved on before the answer arrives. You cannot yet tell which bad seasons are signal and which are ordinary variance, and the redundancy you have on paper may be shared with every other buyer who would switch at the same moment. Over-reacting costs money too.

We do not propose a company-wide climate study. We propose a climate decision capability: a way of analysing climate data that ends in a decision, built on the few crops, sites and products that carry the business, tested against your own history, and left in your hands to re-run. Disclosure draws from the same evidence as a side benefit.

1 · Scope of workWhat each workstream will actually answer

WS1 Sourcing (upstream)

  1. For each priority crop and region: what conditions produce the volume and price we plan on?

WS2 Facilities (operations)

  1. Which perils genuinely affect how each priority site operates? (Thresholds, not damage curves.)

WS3 Demand (downstream)

  1. Is beverage demand moved by extreme heat or cold in a way that is large enough to act on?

2 · MethodologyStep 1: Prioritise, then find the assumption

What we do. Use Larkspur's own numbers to rank crops by revenue and margin, sites by output at risk, and beverage products by volume. Keep the few that carry the business. For each one, extract from contracts, plans and interviews the physical assumption the plan rests on and write it into an assumptions register.

What an assumption looks like (illustrated for a tree crop). Not "Fresno is getting hotter" but "the plan assumes fewer than N days above 35 °C during bloom", "at least X weeks of inventory", "an alternate region that isn't in the same drought", "the line can run when the ambient temperature is under T". Growers, agronomists and facility managers supply the thresholds. The models do not.

Why it comes first. This is where your "signal versus noise" question is decided. A plan with no stated physical assumption cannot be tested; it can only be argued about after a bad season.

Output. Priority list and assumptions register, agreed with procurement, plant and commercial at the week-2 gate.

2 · MethodologyStep 2: Build the physical premise from three layers

LayerWhat it answersSources (illustrated for a Central Valley tree crop)
ProjectionHow the odds of the condition shift over the decision horizonNASA NEX-GDDP-CMIP6, daily, ~25 km, bias-corrected and downscaled. Three models from different families: ACCESS-CM2, MPI-ESM1-2-HR, MRI-ESM2-0
ObservationWhat the condition has actually done, season by seasonStation and gridded records (NOAA, PRISM), US Drought Monitor county series
MeasurementThe managed system that sits between weather and the crop or siteWater allocations (Friant Class 1/2), April 1 snowpack, groundwater levels, reservoir storage where it discriminates

Model selection follows six filters, applied before we look for an answer: variable and mechanism · geography and resolution · scenario and horizon · baseline and comparability · known performance and limits · access and provenance. Model families and shared downscaling limit how independent three models really are; we say so.

Settings, held constant across models. Same variable, same grid cell, same experiment. Baseline historical 1985–2014; near term ssp245 2015–2034; decision horizon ssp245 2036–2065. We count seasons per 30-year window that cross the threshold from Step 1, per model. The result reads "a once-a-decade season becomes once every 2 to 4 years, depending on model", never "2043 will be bad".

One scenario. ssp245, the current-policies pathway, because to 2050 the models and natural variability matter more than the scenario. ssp370 stress and ssp126 Paris-aligned are configuration lines, re-run on request; so is CMIP7 when downscaled data arrives.

WS2 uses the same layers at site coordinates. WS3 uses observation only; no projections are needed to test whether heat moves sales.

2 · MethodologyStep 3: Calibrate against Larkspur's own history

The question. When the condition moved in the past, did Larkspur's numbers move with it? This is the step most climate studies skip, and the reason they never connect to a decision.

How. Match series on date and place; look for relationships that survive simple checks (seasonality, price trend, promotions, one-off events). Written in Python that Larkspur's team can read and re-run.

Two honest outcomes. A relationship large enough to plan on, or a null result. We report the null. A null in WS3 ends that workstream; a null in WS1 usually means the threshold, not the crop, was wrong, and we go back to Step 1.

2 · MethodologyStep 4: Translate into money and time

Scenarios, not forecasts. For each priority item we build three or four scenarios from the physical premise: the condition holds, the condition is breached once, the condition is breached in consecutive seasons, and the condition is breached in the primary and the alternate region at once. At least one scenario is a case where current buffers hold.

Each scenario traces the cascade, in inspectable code, using the calibrated relationships from Step 3:

condition breached → volume / quality / throughput → inventory drawn down → substitute sought
→ approval and cost of substitute → customer commitment met, late or missed → margin, COGS, lost sales

Two questions the cascade must answer.

  • Which channel? Higher input cost, lost or spoiled product, replacement-supply premium, missed retail commitment, extra operating cost at a site, or capital to harden.
  • Which clock? Contracts and retail commitments run for years; inventory and qualified substitutes are finite buffers. The consequence lands when the buffer runs out, not when the weather changes.

Correlated substitution. We test each alternate region and co-manufacturer against the same models. If the alternate shifts the same way in the same seasons, it is not redundancy, and we say how much of Larkspur's paper optionality survives.

What the output is. Conditional consequences under each scenario, with the assumptions listed beside each number. Not an annual expected loss, not a probability-weighted average of three models.

2 · MethodologyStep 5: Compare actions

WorkstreamActions compared
WS1 SourcingRenew as-is · adjust volumes or term · qualify an alternate region now · change inventory weeks · lock price
WS2 FacilitiesOperate as-is · change the operating envelope (shifts, scheduling) · invest to harden · shift allocation to another site or co-manufacturer · renegotiate co-manufacturer terms
WS3 DemandCurrent plan · weather-informed allocation and timing · weather-informed promotion or pricing

Decision rule. Prefer the action that holds up across the model spread and the scenario set, not the one optimised for a single projection. For each recommendation we state the evidence that would change it and the date by which it matters.

What you get. A recommendation per priority item, the assumptions it rests on, the scenarios in which it fails, and the option value of waiting where that is the honest answer.

2 · MethodologyUncertainty: what we rank, what we show, what we don't claim

Ranked, largest first, for decisions to 2050:

  1. The threshold itself (from growers and facility managers)
  2. Spread across the three models
  3. Natural year-to-year variability, hence 30-year windows
  4. Scenario choice

Shown. Sensitivity within the model (thresholds, buffer sizes, substitute cost) as a range. Model spread as three separate lines, never averaged into one.

Named, not banded. Mechanisms we did not model have no place in a range. We list them.

Not claimed. Attribution of any past season to climate change. A forecast for a specific year. A probability distribution from three models. A dollar loss read directly from a temperature. A structural damage estimate for any building.

3 · Assurability and accuracyUncertainty and limits, stated plainly

What we show. Sensitivity as a range. Model spread as three lines. Mechanisms we did not model as a list. A range is not a confidence interval and the tool will say so.

What we do not claim. That any past season was caused by climate change. A forecast for a given year. A probability from three models. Structural damage to any building.

Audit trail is not assurance. Our workpapers let your team and your auditors review the work. They are not independent assurance. Windward advises; it does not assure. Assurance is a separate engagement by a separate firm. We structure workpapers so that engagement is straightforward.

Client information. Larkspur data stays within the engagement. Nothing enters a public AI service without your written authorisation. This coursework uses simulated records only.

A final section maps the outputs to the four IFRS S2 pillars and TCFD's eleven disclosures, states the gaps, and says what a second scenario or assurance-grade workpapers would add if your standard requires them.
